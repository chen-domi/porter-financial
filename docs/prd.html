01Summary

What we're building, for whom, and what "done" means for the MVP.

Problem

My finances are spread across six institutions and seven cards. Each card has its own points currency, statement cycle, and a growing list of monthly, quarterly, and semi-annual credits. No single app shows net worth, card balances, due dates, points, and unused credits together. None of them tell me, at the moment I pay, what that purchase did to my budget.

Product statement

Porter is

A private, iPhone-first finance app for one person. It brings every account into a single calm view, warns me before card value expires, and reflects each purchase back to me in real time. Later it becomes a financial assistant I can talk to.

Goals

G1 · Clarity

Net worth, card balances, statement due dates, and points on one screen, refreshed at least daily.

G2 · No wasted value

Zero card credits expire unused without at least two reminders first.

G3 · Spend awareness

After an Apple Pay purchase, the Dynamic Island shows the remaining weekly/monthly budget within ~5 seconds.

G4 · Engineering

Production-shaped on AWS (IaC, CI/CD, queues, observability) at a baseline under $5/mo of infrastructure, with a full-scale setup that can be switched on to prove it scales.

Non-goals for the MVP

  • Multi-user accounts, sharing, or onboarding for anyone else. The data model stays user-scoped so this could change later.
  • Moving money: no payments, transfers, or card actions. Porter is read-only against institutions.
  • Android or web. Expo keeps both possible later.
  • Scraping issuer websites or storing bank credentials.
  • Crypto and brokerages other than Schwab.
  • Card application or churning recommendations.

04Features & requirements

P0 must ship in the MVP. P1 ships in the MVP if time allows. P2 is post-MVP. Each feature has acceptance criteria.

F1

Account connections

MVP

Link Amex, Chase, Capital One, Bank of America, Bilt, and Schwab through Plaid Link, and Apple Card through FinanceKit.

  • P0Plaid Link (OAuth institutions) from the app; access tokens are exchanged and stored encrypted server-side, never on the device.
  • P0Incremental sync with /transactions/sync and a saved cursor, driven by Plaid webhooks, plus a scheduled safety refresh every 6 hours.
  • P0Liabilities (statement balance, minimum due, due date, APR) and Investments (Schwab holdings and balances).
  • P0Connection health: show "needs re-auth" when an item breaks, with a one-tap fix through Link update mode.
  • P1Apple Card through a FinanceKit native module. Fallback: monthly statement CSV import if the entitlement is delayed.
  • P1A FinancialDataProvider interface, so sandbox fixtures and a second aggregator can plug in without touching domain code.

Accepted when: all six institutions are linked in production, balances are under 24 hours old, and an expired login appears on the dashboard within one sync cycle.

F2

Net worth, cards & payments

MVP
  • P0Net worth = assets (checking, savings, Schwab) − liabilities (card balances), with a daily snapshot table for history (1W / 1M / 3M / 1Y / All).
  • P0Per-card view: current balance, statement balance, credit limit and utilization, due date, minimum due, and last payment.
  • P0Upcoming payments list sorted by due date, with "statement balance covered by checking?" status.
  • P0Transactions list with search, filtering by card or category, and pending/posted state.
  • P1Net worth change explained: "+$1,240 this week: Schwab +$980, cash +$410, cards −$150".
  • P2Optional points value in net worth, using user-set cents-per-point (off by default).

Accepted when: the net worth on screen is within $1 of a manual sum of the institutions' own figures, and every card shows its correct next due date.

F3

Points tracking

MVP

No official API exposes points balances, so Porter combines confirmed balances (entered manually or parsed from emails) with an estimate computed from transactions since the last confirmation.

  • P0Quick manual update for each program (two taps plus number entry), with a "last confirmed" timestamp.
  • P0Estimated balance = last confirmed + points earned since, using the catalog's earn rules applied to posted transactions. Clearly labeled as an estimate.
  • P1Parse balances from issuer emails (statement emails and rewards summaries) when present. Each parsed value updates the confirmed balance and records where it came from.
  • P1Reminder when a confirmed balance is more than 30 days old.

Accepted when: after a month of normal spending, the estimated balance is within 3% of the issuer's actual balance for MR and UR.

F4

Benefits & expiring credits

MVP

The feature that pays for the app. Every credit becomes a benefit period (for example "Gold dining credit, October 2026: $10, $0 used, resets Nov 1"), and Porter detects usage automatically.

  • P0Catalog of benefit definitions per card: amount, cadence (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, calendar-year, or cardmember-year), and whether it needs enrollment or activation.
  • P0Generate benefit periods automatically; cardmember-year benefits use each card's anniversary date.
  • P0Detect usage from (a) the issuer's statement credit transactions (for example "Platinum Digital Entertainment Credit") and (b) qualifying merchant purchases matched by rules. Manual "mark used" as a fallback.
  • P0"Needs attention" on the dashboard: unused credits expiring within 10 days, ordered by value × urgency.
  • P0Push reminders at 7 days and 2 days before expiry (configurable per benefit), plus a monthly "credits reset today" note.
  • P1Activation tasks: Freedom Flex quarterly categories, enrollment-required credits, DashPass. These show as checklist items until done.
  • P1Annual fee scorecard per card: credits used plus estimated rewards value, compared with the annual fee.

Accepted when: over a full quarter, no credit expires unused without a reminder, and auto-detection catches at least 90% of credits actually used.

F5

Budgets & real-time spend

MVP

Weekly and monthly budgets, updated the moment I pay. See §06 for how the data gets there.

  • P0One weekly and one monthly discretionary budget. Rent, card payments, transfers, and excluded categories don't count against them.
  • P0Remaining = budget − (posted + pending + real-time spend events not yet matched to a transaction).
  • P0Apple Pay → Dynamic Island: within ~5 seconds of a tap, a Live Activity shows the card, merchant, amount, and the remaining weekly and monthly budget.
  • P0Non-Apple Pay purchases (physical card, online) arrive as a push notification once an issuer alert email or Plaid reports them.
  • P1Per-category budgets (dining, groceries, shopping, travel) with pace ("on track to finish the month at 112%").
  • P1Card tip in the Live Activity: "Gold would have earned 4× here (+$1.80)".

Accepted when: p95 tap-to-Island latency is under 5 seconds over 20 test purchases, and each purchase counts once against the budget no matter how many sources report it.

F6

Notifications & daily digest

MVP
  • P0Push categories: purchase, benefit expiring, payment due (5 days and 1 day before), connection broken, daily digest. Each can be switched off.
  • P0Daily digest at 8:00 local time: yesterday's spend, budget remaining, credits expiring this week, payments due this week, net worth change.
  • P1Quiet hours; related alerts grouped into one notification.
F7

Personalized offers & deals

Phase 2

Amex Offers, Chase Offers, and Rakuten have no public consumer APIs. Porter ingests offer emails from Gmail, normalizes them, and ranks them against what I actually buy.

  • P1Offer ingestion: parse offer and "offer added" emails into a normalized record (merchant, reward, minimum spend, expiry, card), with an LLM fallback for formats no parser handles yet.
  • P1Quick add: paste or share-sheet an offer from the Amex/Chase app (screenshot → structured offer through vision extraction).
  • P1Relevance v1 (no training data needed): score = merchant affinity (how often and how recently I shop there) + category affinity + offer value − expiry penalty.
  • P2Relevance v2 (learned): logistic regression or a contextual bandit trained on my 👍 / dismiss / "used it" feedback plus transaction features. Retrained weekly.
  • P2Alert when I buy at a merchant with an un-added or unused offer.
F8

AI assistant

Phase 3

Designed now, built after the MVP. See §09. Covers: talk to my finances, a weekly summary, which card to use, and proactive suggestions.

05Experience & design

Premium means atmosphere, materials, and editorial confidence: a private bank's app, not a spreadsheet. Calm, but never plain.

Design direction: "Midnight & metal"

Dark is the default. Deep obsidian surfaces are lit by soft, diffuse light from above, like a room at night. Frosted glass panels sit on top. Display type is an editorial serif; numbers are thin sans. The cards themselves appear as rendered metal objects. The light theme mirrors this with warm paper and pale botanical washes. References: Albert (editorial serif plus washes), Awe (dark glass and glowing charts), LAIN (restraint and photographic mood), and Nova (precious-metal materials).

Light, not gradients

Backgrounds are lit by large, blurred radial light sources at low opacity, so they feel like a surface catching light. Buttons and text stay flat. No linear rainbow fills, ever.

Glass & depth

Three layers: an atmospheric base, frosted glass panels (blur, 1px inner light edge), and solid controls on top. Depth comes from translucency and blur, not drop shadows.

Editorial type

Serif display for greetings, screen titles, and moments ("This week", "Benefits"). Thin sans for numbers; cents set as a muted superscript. Small caps tracked out for labels.

Precious materials

Card art is rendered as metal (brushed platinum, warm gold) with a slow light sweep driven by the gyroscope. Champagne is the one warm accent and marks value: points, credits, rewards.

Data that glows

Charts use thin lines with a soft fill that fades to nothing, one highlighted mark, and a floating tooltip pill. Unhighlighted marks fall back into the surface.

Choreographed motion

Numbers roll, cards lift with spring physics, glass panels blur in. Haptics confirm every meaningful change. Nothing loops or bounces just to decorate.

06Real-time spend pipeline

The technically hardest MVP feature. Three sources report the same purchase at different speeds, and Porter merges them into one spend event.

SourceCoversTypical latencyMechanism
Wallet automationAny Apple Pay purchase, any card~1–5 sShortcuts "Transaction" automation → Porter App Intent → Live Activity + API call
Issuer alert emailAmex, Chase, C1, Bilt purchase alerts (threshold set to $0)~1–3 minGmail filter → forward → SES inbound → parser → push
Plaid webhookEverything linkedMinutes to hoursSYNC_UPDATES_AVAILABLE → /transactions/sync
FinanceKitApple CardNear real time, on deviceNative module reads new transactions, posts them to the API

Apple Pay → Dynamic Island

  1. One-time setup. I create a Shortcuts personal automation: When I tap any Wallet card → Run "Log Purchase" (Porter), set to Run Immediately. iOS doesn't allow an app to create this automation itself, so Porter ships a guided setup screen and a shared Shortcut.
  2. App Intent runs. Porter exposes a Swift LiveActivityIntent that receives card, merchant, and amount. It runs in the background without opening the app.
  3. Instant local math. The intent reads the cached budget state (App Group storage), subtracts the amount, and starts or updates the Live Activity. This step doesn't wait on the network.
  4. Durable record. The intent POSTs a spend_event (idempotency key = hash of card, amount, and timestamp) to the API, which recomputes authoritative budgets and sends a Live Activity push-update if the result differs.
  5. Reconcile. When the email alert or Plaid transaction arrives, the matcher links it to the existing spend event instead of counting it twice.

Reconciliation rules

  • Match on account/card + amount + time window (72 h) + fuzzy merchant name. Restaurants allow up to +30% for tips, because the pending amount differs from the posted one.
  • A spend event moves through realtime → pending → posted. Any unmatched real-time event is resolved after 5 days.
  • Each spend event keeps a provenance list of every source that reported it. This is useful for debugging and later for AI citations.
Native code required

Live Activities, App Intents, and FinanceKit need Swift targets. Porter uses Expo dev builds (not Expo Go) with a widget extension (for example via @bacons/apple-targets) and a local Expo module for the intent and FinanceKit. Live Activity push updates go to APNs directly, because Expo's push service doesn't support the liveactivity push type.

Backend modules

identity · connections (Plaid, FinanceKit ingest) · ledger (accounts, transactions, spend events, reconciliation) · networth · catalog (cards, earn rules, benefit definitions) · benefits · points · budgets · inbox (email ingestion and parsers) · notifications · offers · assistant.

Modules talk through service interfaces and domain events written to a transactional outbox table. The API publishes them to SQS right after commit, and an EventBridge sweeper re-publishes anything left behind every minute. A crash can never leave a transaction stored without its follow-up work.

Why serverless + Neon

Avoid the NAT trap

The obvious all-AWS design (Lambda in a private network talking to RDS) needs a NAT Gateway so Lambda can reach Plaid, APNs, and Claude. That alone costs about $32/mo, more than everything else combined. Porter's Lambdas stay outside a VPC and reach Neon over TLS, so there's no NAT, no idle database, and no idle servers. Aurora Serverless v2 with the Data API is the all-AWS alternative if Neon's limits are ever reached.

  • Latency: Lambda cold starts (about 1 s for a container image) don't affect the Dynamic Island, which is computed on the device. Plaid webhooks only need a response within seconds.
  • Portability: the FastAPI app knows nothing about Lambda. Mangum adapts it, and the same image runs under uvicorn on Fargate or locally.

Email ingestion (Gmail)

  1. Gmail filters (from Amex, Chase, Capital One, Bilt, Rakuten) auto-forward to in@mail.<porter-domain>.
  2. An SES receipt rule stores the raw MIME in encrypted S3 and enqueues a job.
  3. The worker tries a template parser first (deterministic, unit-tested against real fixtures). If no parser matches, Claude extracts fields into a strict JSON schema with a confidence score.
  4. Low-confidence results go to an in-app "Review" inbox and are never applied silently.

Why forwarding instead of the Gmail API: gmail.readonly is a restricted scope. An unverified Google app in "testing" mode has refresh tokens that expire every 7 days. Forwarding avoids both problems and keeps all ingestion inside AWS.

09AI-native, by design

Built after the MVP, but the MVP's data shapes are chosen with it in mind. The model explains results the system has computed; it never invents the numbers.

Ask anything

"Did I use my Uber credit this month?" "How much did I spend on dining vs. last October?" Chat over typed, read-only tools; answers cite the transactions and benefits they're based on.

Weekly summary

Sunday evening: spend vs. budget, net worth change, value captured from credits, what's expiring, one suggestion. Written from computed facts.

Which card?

A deterministic earn-rate engine (catalog + caps + quarterly categories + offers) ranks cards; the AI explains the choice. Available in the app and the Live Activity.

Proactive suggestions

Detectors flag unused credits, wrong-card purchases, subscription price increases, and budget pace. The AI writes the alert; detectors decide whether it fires.

What the MVP does now to enable this

  • Typed read APIs for every domain (accounts, transactions, benefits, budgets, points). These become the assistant's tools with no rework.
  • Provenance everywhere: spend event sources, parsed email IDs, catalog versions. Citations come for free.
  • Clean merchant normalization and categories. Most of an AI assistant's quality comes from good data.
  • An insight table with detector name, facts JSON, and status, populated by deterministic rules in the MVP.
  • The Claude API is already used for email extraction, so prompts, evaluations, cost limits, and logging are in place before chat ships.

Guardrails

  • Read-only tools. No action that changes anything without explicit confirmation in the UI.
  • Account numbers and personal details are stripped before prompts; only the needed rows are sent.
  • An evaluation set of about 50 real questions with expected answers runs in CI whenever prompts change.
  • A monthly token budget with a hard cap and alerting.

11Infrastructure, delivery & cost

Cheap by default, scalable on demand, and the evidence that it scales is part of the deliverable.

Delivery pipeline

  • Pull request: ruff, mypy, pytest (against a real Postgres), OpenAPI diff, eslint/tsc, Terraform plan.
  • Merge to main: build an ARM container image, push to ECR, terraform apply to staging (a Neon branch), run Alembic migrations, smoke tests.
  • Promote: the same image to prod with a canary on the Lambda alias (10% for 5 minutes) and automatic rollback on error alarms.
  • Preview databases: every pull request gets a free Neon database branch, so migrations are tested against a copy of real data.
  • Mobile: EAS Build and TestFlight; EAS Update for JavaScript-only changes.
  • Operate: CloudWatch alarms (5xx rate, DLQ depth, sync lag, budget) and OpenTelemetry traces sent to Grafana Cloud's free tier.

  3. Dashboards and results are exported to docs/benchmarks/.
  4. terraform destroy. A few hours of it costs about $1.

This gives the résumé a measured claim, for example "serverless or containers from one codebase via IaC; load-tested to N req/s; $3/mo AWS baseline", instead of a bill for idle servers.

15Open questions
